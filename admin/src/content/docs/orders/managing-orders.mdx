**Orders** is the page you'll have open. It's **live**: a new order appears
without a refresh, so the page can sit on a second screen during trading hours.

## Working an order

<Steps>
  <Step title="It arrives as Pending">
    Paid and waiting on you. Open it and check you can fulfil every line — a
    substitution is a conversation, not a status.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mark it On the way">
    Sets it to in-process and notifies the shopper. Do this when you actually
    start, not when you intend to — the notification says it's moving.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hand it over">
    Ask the shopper for their delivery code and check it against the one on the
    order.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mark it Delivered">
    Terminal. It can't be cancelled afterwards, and the shopper can now rate it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning">
  Don't mark orders delivered in a batch at end of day. Each one sends a
  "delivered" push at the moment you click, and a shopper who got their bag at
  noon receiving that notification at 9pm reads it as a mistake.
</Callout>

## What's on an order

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Items** | Name, image, quantity, size and price, as sold. |
| **Delivery address** | As it was at placement — later edits don't move it. |
| **Total** | Items − coupon + delivery fee. Each shown separately. |
| **Coupon** | The code used, and what it took off. |
| **Delivery code** | The handover OTP. |
| **Payment** | Which provider, and the payment reference for support lookups. |
| **Timeline** | Every status change, dated. |
| **Rating** | The shopper's rating of the delivery, once given. |

The payment reference is what you paste into your Razorpay or Stripe dashboard
when you need to trace a charge. The order also records **which** provider took
the money, so you know which dashboard to open.

## Finding an order

**Search orders** matches across the order's details, so a shopper's name, a
phone number or an order id all work. Status filters narrow to Pending, On the
way, Delivered or Cancelled.

The workflow that matters: filter to **Pending** each morning. That's your
queue.

## Order ratings

Once delivered, a shopper can rate the order 1–5 with optional text — how the
*delivery* went, which is a different question from what they thought of a
product.

It appears on the order in the console. It's a signal, not something to manage:
there's no reply and no moderation, because it isn't public. Product reviews
are the public ones — see [Product reviews](/docs/growing/product-reviews).

## A daily rhythm

<Steps>
  <Step title="Morning: clear Pending">
    Filter to Pending. Accept what you can fulfil, cancel what you can't
    (which refunds automatically).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Through the day: advance as you go">
    Mark on the way when you start; delivered at the door.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evening: check for stragglers">
    Anything still Pending from this morning is an order the shopper thinks you
    forgot. Usually they're right.
  </Step>
</Steps>
