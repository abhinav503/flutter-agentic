Banners are the carousel at the top of your storefront's home screen. They're
the first thing a shopper sees after opening your store, and the only surface
where you choose what to show rather than the catalog choosing for you.

Manage them on the **Banners** page.

## The fields

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Image** | The artwork. Landscape. |
| **Title** | Overlaid on the banner. |
| **Subtitle** | A supporting line under the title. |
| **Tapping it opens** | Nothing, a product, or a category. |
| **Position** | Sort order in the carousel. Lower is earlier. |
| **Active** | Off keeps the banner without showing it. |

## Artwork

Landscape, and readable at phone width. Templates render banners full-bleed or
as a peeking card depending on the template, so:

- Keep the **subject centred**. Edges get cropped differently per template.
- Leave the **left third emptier** — that's where the title and subtitle sit.
- Avoid baking text into the image. It won't match the overlaid title's font,
  it can't be edited, and it won't translate for a shopper reading in another
  language.

<Callout type="tip">
  Photography of the actual products beats an abstract graphic. A shopper
  scanning a carousel is deciding whether you sell what they want.
</Callout>

## What a tap does

Three options:

- **Nothing (display only)** — announcements, hours, service messages.
- **A product** — opens that product's details screen. Right for a single
  hero item.
- **A category** — opens that category. Right for a sale across a range.

<Callout type="warning">
  A banner advertising a discount should link to the thing that's discounted.
  A "40% off fruit" banner that opens nothing makes a shopper hunt for the
  offer, and most won't.
</Callout>

## Position and how many

**Position** orders the carousel — lower numbers first. Leave gaps (10, 20, 30)
so you can slot one in later without renumbering everything.

Three to five active banners is the useful range. One is a static image nobody
registers as a carousel; ten means the last five are never seen.

## Active, instead of delete

Toggling **Active** off keeps the banner — its artwork, copy and target — but
removes it from the carousel. That's what you want for anything seasonal: a
festival banner switched off in January and on again next year beats recreating
it annually.

Delete only what you'll never run again.

## A rhythm that works

<Steps>
  <Step title="One evergreen">
    What your store is, always on. New shoppers see it whenever they arrive.
  </Step>
  <Step title="One current offer">
    Linked to the category or product it discounts. Swap it when the offer ends.
  </Step>
  <Step title="One seasonal">
    Switched on and off by date. Reused every year.
  </Step>
</Steps>
