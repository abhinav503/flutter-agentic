Categories are how shoppers navigate your store. The home screen, the browse
tab and the category grid are all built from them, which is why a store needs
**at least one** before it can be previewed.

Create them on the **Categories** page.

## The fields

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | What shoppers see. Keep it short — templates render it in two lines at most. |
| **Group** | An optional heading that gathers related categories together. |
| **Image** | The tile artwork on the browse grid. |

## Parent categories

A category can sit under another — *Clothing › Shirts* — by picking a
**Parent category** in its dialog. Imports from Shopify, WooCommerce and
WhatsApp catalogs build this tree automatically from their category paths.
Products always attach to the category itself (usually the leaf); the tree is
for organisation and for keeping an imported catalog's structure.

## Groups

The **Group** field is the difference between a flat list of twenty categories
and a browsable store. Categories sharing a group name are shown together under
that heading.

```text
Group: Grocery & Kitchen
  Fresh Fruits
  Vegetables
  Dairy, Bread & Eggs
  Atta, Rice & Dal

Group: Snacks & Drinks
  Chips & Namkeen
  Cold Drinks & Juices
  Tea, Coffee & More
```

Groups aren't records you create — they're just a name you type on each
category, and matching names gather. That means a typo makes a second group, so
copy the exact spelling when you add the fourth category to a group.

<Callout type="tip">
  Leave Group empty if you have fewer than about eight categories. Grouping
  three things under two headings adds a layer without adding navigation.
</Callout>

## Category images

Each category shows a tile image on the browse grid. Square artwork works best;
templates crop to a square and centre it.

A representative *product photo* usually reads better at tile size than an icon
or an illustration — a shopper scanning for "Dairy" recognises a milk carton
faster than a dairy glyph.

## Naming

Category names are one of the few things shoppers read before they've decided
to trust you.

- Say what's inside: **Fresh Fruits**, not **Category 1**.
- Match how shoppers speak, not how your supplier invoices.
- Two words beats five. **Cold Drinks** beats **Cold Beverages & Soft Drinks**,
  which will clip.

## Deleting a category

Deleted categories sit in **Recently deleted** for 30 days and can be
restored with one tap.


Deleting asks for confirmation and can't be undone.

<Callout type="warning">
  Products belonging **only** to the deleted category become unreachable by
  browsing. They still exist, still appear in search, and can still be opened
  from a direct link — but nobody navigating your aisles will find them.
  Re-assign those products before deleting, or delete them too.
</Callout>

## Import instead

Migrating a real store? Categories have a CSV importer of their own, with the
same validate-everything-first behaviour as
[the product importer](/docs/catalog/import-products-csv). Do categories first,
then brands, then products — products reference the other two **by name**, and
the importer won't invent a name that doesn't exist.
