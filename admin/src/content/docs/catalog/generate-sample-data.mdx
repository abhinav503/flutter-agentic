An empty store is hard to judge. **Settings → Sample data → Generate sample
data** fills one with a realistic grocery catalog so you can walk your
storefront, test checkout, and see how a template actually looks with products
in it.

## What it writes

Between 155 and 200 records depending on the market, in a single atomic
batch:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Categories** | 10 grouped aisles, in every market |
| **Brands** | 32 to 71 real brands with logos |
| **Products** | 96 to 111, priced, categorised and stocked |
| **Coupons** | 6 or 7 working discount codes |
| **Banners** | 4 home-screen promos |

Product photography is real, sourced from [Open Food
Facts](https://world.openfoodfacts.org) and used under CC-BY-SA — the
attribution is shown in the dialog itself.

## Pick your market

Seven catalogs ship, and the picker defaults to the one matching your store's
currency:

| Market | Catalog |
| --- | --- |
| **India** | Quick-commerce aisles — Amul, Britannia, Tata, Maggi. In rupees. |
| **Germany** | German-language aisles — Kerrygold, Dr. Oetker, Ritter Sport, Haribo. In euros. |
| **France** | French-language aisles — Président, Bonne Maman, LU, Evian. In euros. |
| **Spain** | Spanish-language aisles — Central Lechera Asturiana, Carbonell, Gullón. In euros. |
| **Italy** | Italian-language aisles — Barilla, Mulino Bianco, Galbani, Lavazza. In euros. |
| **United Kingdom** | Warburtons, Heinz, Cathedral City, Yorkshire Tea, Cadbury. In pounds. |
| **United States** | Cheerios, Kraft, Chobani, DiGiorno, Folgers. In dollars. |

The default is a default, not a lock. Euro alone doesn't identify a country,
and a catalog's brands and language are as market-specific as its prices — so
choose the one you actually operate in.

<Callout type="warning">
  The prices are that market's realistic prices, in that market's currency. If
  your store's currency differs from the market you generate, every price will
  be wrong for your store. Match them.
</Callout>

## Before you generate

Sample data only works on an **empty** store — no products, no categories. A
store that already has either is refused, so a second click can't double
everything. Delete what's there first (or start from a fresh store), or skip
sample data and import your real catalog.


<Callout type="error">
  This is for **exploring the console**, not for going live. Publishing a store
  full of generated products means selling things you don't stock, at prices
  you didn't set, with photos you don't own the rights to use commercially.
</Callout>

Generating into a store that already has products is **warned about but not
blocked** — items are added alongside what's there, never replacing it. The
recovery story is per-item delete, which is tedious for ninety products. Prefer
generating into an empty store.

## Using it well

<Steps>
  <Step title="Generate into a new store">
    Make a store specifically to explore in. Keep your real one clean.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Walk the storefront">
    Open it in the app and browse it properly. This is the fastest way to
    choose between templates — a real catalog shows you what a description
    can't.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test checkout end to end">
    With [test payment keys](/docs/payments/connect-razorpay), place an order,
    advance it through the console, cancel one, refund it. Every flow works
    against generated data.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start your real catalog clean">
    When you know what you want, build the real store by hand or
    [import a CSV](/docs/catalog/import-products-csv).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Accordion title="Can I use the generated products as a starting point?">
  You can edit them like any other product, but you're editing someone else's
  brand names and photography. For a real catalog, exporting the shape into a
  spreadsheet and importing your own rows is cleaner than overwriting ninety
  records one at a time.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Nothing was written and I got an error">
  The whole seed is one atomic batch: it either writes everything or nothing,
  so a failure never leaves a half-seeded store. Re-run it. If it keeps
  failing, check that your store is selected in the switcher and that you're
  still signed in.
</Accordion>
